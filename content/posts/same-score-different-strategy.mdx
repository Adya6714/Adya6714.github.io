Large language models are routinely ranked by a single accuracy number. That number is useful, and also dangerously incomplete.

When two systems land on the same score, we often treat them as interchangeable. In [Same Score, Different Strategy](/research#same-score), we show they are not: trajectory-level probes reveal systematically different solution strategies, failure modes, and sensitivity to prompt framing.

## The evaluation gap

Benchmark accuracy collapses a high-dimensional behavior into a scalar. That collapse hides:

1. **Path dependence** — how the model arrives at an answer
2. **Constraint violations** mid-chain that later self-correct
3. **Strategy switches** under minor paraphrase

Consider a multi-step arithmetic-plus-logic item. Model A may keep an explicit intermediate invariant; Model B may pattern-match the final format. Both can be “correct.”

$$
\text{Acc}(f) = \mathbb{E}_{x \sim \mathcal{D}}[\mathbf{1}[f(x)=y]]
$$

Accuracy alone cannot distinguish these regimes.

## What we measure instead

We introduce complementary probes that score *strategy*, not only outcome. Details live in the constellation panel and the full paper artifact.

<Callout type="idea">
Evaluation that stops at accuracy will over-trust fluent systems and under-credit brittle-but-principled ones.
</Callout>

## Takeaway

If your leaderboard cannot tell these models apart, your deployment decisions are underdetermined. Strategy-aware evaluation is not optional polish — it is the difference between measuring performance and understanding it.
